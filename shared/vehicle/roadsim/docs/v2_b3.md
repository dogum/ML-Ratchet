# RoadSim physics v2, work package B3: vehicle dynamics

Sub-second integration, a tyre model, a human driver and a better wind. Every feature is an option in
`roadsim/options.py` (section "dynamics"). Defaults reproduce v1 exactly: the 171-run fingerprint matches to 1e-9 and
the default path runs at the same speed (within ±5 %, timing noise). The preset `roadsim-physics-v2-b3` switches on
the values below for the calibration pass. Code: `sim.py` (loop), `tyre.py`, `driver.py`, `wind.py`; tests:
`tests/test_roadsim_b3.py` (12 tests, ~17 s).

| option | default (v1) | B3 preset | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `substeps` | 1 | 10 | integration steps per trip second |
| `substep_record` | False | False | also return the 10 Hz arrays in `out['hf']` |
| `tyre_model` | False | True | load transfer, friction ellipse, slip, physics regen limit, EU Crr class, ice |
| `tyre_mu_scale` | 1.0 | 1.0 | multiplier on the peak-friction table |
| `tyre_regen_mu_frac` | 0.8 | 0.8 | share of the driven axle's friction that regen may use |
| `tyre_ref_eu_class` | 'C' | 'C' | EU class the spec's Crr stands for |
| `driver_model` | 'pi' | 'pi' | 'pi' = v1 follower, 'human' = lag, anticipation, overshoot |
| `driver_aggressiveness` | 0.5 | – | 0 eco … 1 aggressive (human driver only) |
| `driver_reaction_s` | 0.5 s | – | perception-reaction delay (human driver only) |
| `wind_profile` | 'none' | 'log' | carry the 10 m station wind down to the vehicle |
| `wind_z0_ref_m` | 0.03 m | 0.03 m | roughness at the station (open airport) |
| `wind_z0_site_m` | 0.1 m | 0.1 m | roughness around the road |
| `wind_shelter` | 1.0 | 1.0 | sheltering factor on the road-height mean wind (or trip array `wind_shelter`) |
| `wind_gusts` | False | True | seeded turbulence along and across the mean wind |
| `wind_turb_seed` | 0 | 0 | turbulence realisation |
| `drafting` | False | True | CdA reduction behind a lead vehicle (acts only if the trip has `gap_m` or `draft_headway_s` is set) |
| `draft_headway_s` | None | None | time gap to an assumed lead vehicle |

The human driver stays off in the preset: it changes the speed trace, so it is an input (who drives), not physics.

## 1. Sub-steps (`substeps`)

The loop runs over integration steps of `dt / substeps`. Trip inputs stay 1 Hz: the target speed is interpolated
linearly inside each second; weather, grade and surface are held. The v1 driver is used in its continuous form:
target slope + the speed error closed over one trip second + the same PI (at `substeps = 1` this is v1's expression,
bit for bit). The powertrain and the strategy are called every sub-step, so shifts, engine starts and stops, the
converter and clutch states, regen blending and traction limits change inside the second. Per-second outputs are
the means of the sub-steps for interval quantities (fuel rate, powers, forces, rpm, `eng_on` becomes the fraction of
the second the engine ran) and the end-of-second value for states (`v`, `soc`, temperatures, `gear`, `v_target`).
`substep_record` adds `out['hf']` with every array at 10 Hz plus `hf['t']`.

One v1 assumption had to change: the engine-start fuel `start_W` was a power booked over the one step in which the
engine starts (i.e. start_W × 1 s). With sub-steps it is booked over the first second of running, so a start costs
the same energy at any step size.

**Is 1 Hz accurate?** Same physics at 1 Hz and 10 Hz, 40 generated light-duty vehicles (all architectures), warm:

| cycle | median | mean abs | 90th pct abs | max abs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UDDS | −0.01 % | 0.11 % | 0.19 % | 0.81 % (a P2 hybrid; P2 median +0.73 %) |
| HWFET | +0.01 % | 0.04 % | 0.13 % | 0.33 % |
| US06 | +0.06 % | 0.09 % | 0.15 % | 0.37 % |

So v1's 1 Hz integration error on trip energy is about 0.1 %, up to ~0.8 % for P2 hybrids in the city (launch clutch
and engine starts land inside the second). The 10 Hz mode matters for what happens inside a second (OBD-like
signals, drivability), not for trip energy. Counts on UDDS / US06 (warm): AT8 car 173 → 173 / 146 → 150 shifts; P2
hybrid 35 → 36 / 44 → 45 engine starts; power-split 35 → 39 / 42 → 43 starts.

**Speed** (single thread, this machine, `record=True`; x real time):

| vehicle | 1 Hz (v1 path) | 10 Hz | B3 preset (10 Hz, all on) |
|---|---|---|---|
| car, conventional (UDDS / VED route) | 50,000 / 47,000 | 4,900 / 4,800 | 3,900 / 4,000 |
| power-split hybrid, A-ECMS | 3,650 / 3,730 | 360 / 350 | 350 / 340 |
| BEV | 49,000 / 47,000 | 4,500 / 4,700 | 3,550 / 3,800 |

10 Hz costs 10× (every sub-step is a full powertrain plan). A hybrid at 350× real time still runs a 30-minute trip in
5 s. The v1 default path: 52,000× (conv UDDS) vs 53,000× before the change.

## 2. Tyre model (`tyre_model`)

* **Axle loads** with grade and acceleration (Gillespie 1992, ch. 2):
  `N_f = m g (b cosθ − h sinθ)/L − m a h/L`, `N_r = m g (a_cg cosθ + h sinθ)/L + m a h/L` (each axle ≥ 5 % of the weight).
* **Friction ellipse**: an axle carries at most `N √(μ² − (a_y/g)²)` longitudinally; `a_y = v·dψ/dt` from the trip
  heading (3 s smoothing, ≤ 30 °/s). Cycles have no heading change, so a_y = 0 there.
  * traction ≤ that limit on the driven axle(s) (FWD, RWD, AWD);
  * friction braking (all wheels, ABS) ≤ that limit on the whole vehicle;
  * **regen ≤ `tyre_regen_mu_frac` × that limit on the driven axle.** This replaces v1's fixed `regen.max_decel_g`
    (0.30 g of the whole vehicle, the same for every drive layout). On dry asphalt at −3 m/s² it allows 0.48 g on a
    front-drive car and 0.32 g on a rear-drive one; on packed snow 0.13 g and 0.09 g.
* **Peak friction** `tyre.MU_PEAK` by surface: dry asphalt 0.90, concrete 0.90, wet 0.60, gravel 0.60, packed snow
  0.25, ice 0.10 (Wong, *Theory of Ground Vehicles* 2008, table 1.3). Surface code 5 = ice is new (v1 clips the
  surface to 0-4, so v1 treats it as snow); ice rolling resistance factor 1.0.
* **Longitudinal slip**: brush model with a parabolic pressure distribution (Pacejka, *Tire and Vehicle Dynamics*
  2012, sec. 3.2), closed-form inverse: `σ = (1 − (1 − |F|/μN)^(1/3)) / θ`, `θ = C_x/(3μN)`, `κ = σ/(1 − σ)`.
  Slip stiffness `C_x = 15 N` (cars; `8 N` heavy duty), × √(width/205 mm) if `tire.size` is given. The powertrain
  is asked for the force at the wheel-rim speed `u (1 + κ)`; the difference `F u κ` is booked in the new ledger term
  **`tyre_slip`** (also the friction brakes' share of slip, moved out of `brake`). At 2 kN on a 9.4 kN driven axle
  (dry) κ = 1.6 %.
* **Cornering drag** `m a_y² / (g C_α)`, `C_α = 11.5 N/rad` (cars; 6 heavy duty): a resisting force in the momentum
  balance, also booked in `tyre_slip`. Only real routes with heading changes see it.
* **EU tyre label classes** (Regulation (EU) 2020/740): `spec['tire']['eu_class']` = 'A'…'E' rescales Crr by the class
  mid-point over the reference class (`tyre_ref_eu_class`, 'C'): A 6.0, B 7.15, C 8.4, D 9.8, E 11.3 kg/t for cars
  (C1); 3.7…7.6 kg/t for trucks and buses (C3). A → E is +88 % Crr. On HWFET (warm) the default car uses +7.7 % (C)
and +17 % (E) more fuel than on A tyres, the BEV +12.5 % and +28 % more energy: rolling resistance is ~45 % of the
default car's road load at 80 km/h. The whole spec Crr is scaled, including the few per cent of bearing and brake
drag in a coast-down A term; the calibration pass may want a non-tyre share.
* **New spec fields** (read with defaults inside `tyre.py`; vehicle.py is unchanged): `spec['chassis'] =
  {cg_height_m, wheelbase_m, front_axle_load_frac, drive, height_m}` (defaults by class, e.g. car 0.55 m, 2.70 m, 0.60,
  FWD, 1.45 m; car/SUV BEV 0.50 m, 0.50, RWD; the Class 8 tractor-trailer folded into two axles with 42 % on the
  drive tandem), `spec['tire'] = {eu_class, size ('205/55R16'), slip_stiffness_per_N, cornering_stiffness_per_N_rad}`.

## 3. Human driver (`driver_model: 'human'`)

The driver sees its speed and the target `τ` late (`driver_reaction_s`, 0.5 s) and combines three wishes:
track the target `T_p` ahead (`(v_tgt(t'+T_p) − v_s)/T_p`), head for the top speed of the next `T_up` seconds
(`(max v_tgt − v_s − db)/T_g`; aggressive drivers rush there), and be at every lower coming speed within `T_dn` in
time (`min (v_tgt(t'+s) − v_s + db)/s`, s ≥ 1 s; eco drivers look 5 s ahead and ease off early). The command is
clipped to comfort limits, slewed by a jerk limit and passed through a pedal/brake lag `τ_a`. A 0.3 m/s perception
deadband stops it chasing trace noise. At a stop it waits `τ` after the target moves; near zero target it brakes to a
halt. Sources for the numbers are in `driver.py` (Bosetti et al. 2014; Murphey et al. 2009; Green 2000).

| aggressiveness | T_p | T_up / T_g | T_dn | a_max / a_min | jerk | τ_a |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 eco | 3.0 s | off / 3.0 s | 5.0 s | 1.5 / −2.5 m/s² | 1.5 m/s³ | 0.35 s |
| 0.5 typical | 1.6 s | 2 s / 2.0 s | 2.5 s | 2.6 / −4.0 | 4 | 0.25 |
| 1 aggressive | 0.8 s | 6 s / 1.0 s | 1.5 s | 4.0 / −6.0 | 10 | 0.15 |

What the parameter spans (car, 10 Hz, UDDS; the trace itself has RPA 0.175 m/s², PKE 0.350 m/s²):

| aggressiveness | RPA m/s² | PKE m/s² | peak accel | distance | trace RMS | fuel per km vs v1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.161 | 0.323 | 1.50 | −2.3 % | 0.48 m/s | −2.4 % |
| 0.25 | 0.164 | 0.329 | 2.01 | −0.6 % | 0.24 | −1.9 % |
| 0.5 | 0.169 | 0.338 | 2.08 | +0.2 % | 0.15 | −1.0 % |
| 0.75 | 0.168 | 0.336 | 3.25 | +2.4 % | 0.45 | +0.4 % |
| 1 | 0.212 | 0.424 | 3.81 | +3.6 % | 0.68 | +15.5 % |

On US06 the span is −8 % (eco, who cannot follow the 3.8 m/s² peaks) to +11 % per km. VED dev trips (looked at,
not fitted; 933 trips ≥ 3 km): RPA 10th/50th/90th percentile 0.13 / 0.18 / 0.23 m/s², PKE 0.26 / 0.37 / 0.47 m/s².
The driver parameter spans PKE 0.32–0.42 on UDDS, i.e. roughly the 25th–80th percentile of VED; the extremes of VED
also come from routes, not only from drivers. Step response 0 → 20 m/s at 2 m/s² (10 Hz): overshoot 0 / 0.09 /
0.35 m/s for eco / typical / aggressive. At 1 Hz the 0.5 s delay is coarse and the aggressive driver overshoots
1.1 m/s: use the human driver with `substeps` ≥ 5.

## 4. Wind (`wind_profile`, `wind_gusts`, `drafting`)

* **Road-height wind** (EN 1991-1-4 sec. 4.3): `v_b = U10 / (k_r(z0_ref) ln(10/z0_ref))`,
  `U(z) = k_r(z0_site) ln(max(z, z_min)/z0_site) v_b`, `k_r = 0.19 (z0/0.05)^0.07`, `z_min` from EN table 4.1
  (1 m at z0 ≤ 0.01 m … 10 m at 1 m), `z = 0.6 ×` vehicle height. A car on a road with hedges (z0 0.1 m) sees 0.64 of
  the airport wind; in suburbs (0.3 m) 0.57; in a city (1 m) 0.51. Times `wind_shelter`.
* **Gusts** (EN 1991-1-4 annex B spectrum, `f S/σ² = 6.8 f_L/(1+10.2 f_L)^(5/3)`): `σ_u = k_r(z0_site) v_b`
  (≈ 0.19 U10 at z0 0.1), `σ_v = 0.75 σ_u`, `L_u = 300 (z/200)^(0.67+0.05 ln z0)` (≈ 30 m), `L_v = 0.35 L_u`. A frozen
  field along the air-relative path (vehicle speed + mean wind), block-averaged over each step: a 1 Hz run sees the
  1-second means, a 10 Hz run the faster gusts. Gusts add drag through the square (`σ²/(v+w)²`) and through yaw.
* **Drafting**: `CdA × (1 − 0.45/(1 + gap/g0)^1.5)`, `g0` = 6 m (light), 15 m (heavy): −10 % at 10 m and −1.6 % at
  50 m for cars, −21 % at 10 m for trucks (Zabat et al. 1995; McAuliffe et al. 2018). Gap from trip `gap_m`
  (`make_trip(..., gap_m=...)`, NaN = no lead) or `draft_headway_s × v + 2 m`.

## 6. Checks

* Fingerprint (all options off): 171 runs match v1 to 1e-9 (`checks/fingerprint.py check`, `tests/test_roadsim_fingerprint.py`).
* Ledger stress, options on: 240 runs, generated vehicles of every class and architecture, 8 cycles, −25…40 °C,
  grades to ±8 %, wind to 12 m/s, all six surfaces, random mixes of sub-steps 1/5/10, tyre, human driver (random
  aggressiveness and delay), wind profile and roughness, gusts, drafting, the preset. Closure median 2e-15, max
  4.0e-4, none over 5e-4, no NaN/inf. The worst run (a conventional SUV on the HD transient cycle with an aggressive
  human driver at 1 Hz) has a 10 kW per-step mismatch; the same run is 5e-16 with the PI driver and 6e-6 at 10 Hz. It
  sits in v1's powertrain plan at the power limit (v1's own 600-run stress worst is 5.1e-4), not in a B3 term.
* `tests/test_roadsim_b3.py`: v1 equality with explicit v1 values, 10 Hz vs 1 Hz, sub-step aggregation, shifts inside
  seconds, slip and load transfer, snow/ice limits on regen and traction, EU classes, human-driver ordering and
  overshoot, wind profile, deterministic seeded gusts, drafting, preset closure, bad option values.

## 7. Still missing

* Shift and start transients: sub-steps place a shift or a start inside the second, but the shift itself is still
  instantaneous (no torque and inertia phase), so 10 Hz rpm jumps by ~1,000 rpm in one step. That belongs to the
  powertrain (B1).
* Tyre size does not change the wheel radius (`wheel_radius_m` in vehicle.py, B2); `tyre.tyre_size()` returns the
  radius for whoever adds it. No tyre temperature or wear effect on μ, no hydroplaning, no aero lift on axle loads,
  no lateral load transfer; slip stiffness does not fall on snow/ice.
* The driver anchors are literature ranges, not fitted. The calibration pass can fit aggressiveness per VED driver on
  dev data (RPA/PKE and trace-following). Real eco driving changes the route time too; on a fixed time-indexed trace
  the eco driver covers 1–3 % less distance, so compare per km.
* Gusts have no time decorrelation of the frozen field and no traffic-induced turbulence; sheltering is a factor, not
  from map data; drafting has no gap data from real trips yet.
* DP and MPC still plan per second; at 10 Hz the sub-steps of a second share that second's policy.
* `wind_profile` + `drafting` interact with v1's calibrated coast-down road loads only through the wind term; EPA
  coast-downs are corrected to zero wind, so the cycle checks are unaffected unless a wind is given.
