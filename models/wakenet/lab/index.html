WakeNet: who is stealing whose wind?

From a wind farm's coordinates, tower heights, rotor curves, nacelle yaw angles, and the wind measured on its unwaked front row, WakeNet reads every downstream turbine's wake power loss and 68% error bar. It trained on 0 real wake labels — only procedural 5-to-16-turbine wind farms laid into real unwaked turbulence — and was graded once on 24,035 real 10-minute SCADA snapshots across two UK wind farms, including a 14-turbine farm it never saw.

68% error-bar coverage: Kelmarsh waked turbines 72.2%, Kelmarsh farm wake loss 77.4%; Penmanshiel waked turbines 50.7%, Penmanshiel farm wake loss 61.5%. On Penmanshiel's 48 m coastal ridge and adjacent forestry blocks, hilltop wind speedup accelerates ridge-top turbines (T12–T15) well above coastal-slope front-row units, adding terrain variance that gentle farmland hills do not teach.

Limits & credits

  • Complex coastal ridges & forestry: Penmanshiel's 48 m relief and forest edges speed hilltop rotors up past front-row power, causing losses vs constant baselines on multi-wake interior and farm-total rows and narrowing 68% bar coverage to 51%.
  • Atmospheric stability is unobserved: Nacelle 10-min SCADA carries no Obukhov heat-flux sensor, leaving convective vs nocturnal stable wake decay inside the residual bar.
