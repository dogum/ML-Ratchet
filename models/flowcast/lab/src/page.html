FlowCast / Hydrology

How much water is in a river nobody measures?

A 25,000-weight LSTM that learned only from simulated rivers, graded once on 619 real US rivers. Pick one of three real exam rivers and see two years of its observed flow next to FlowCast’s prediction. Change the rain, the temperature or what the network is told about the basin, and it reruns on all 731 days in your browser.

Final exam · 619 CAMELS-US rivers · WY1990–99, and 603 rivers WY2016–23 · run once

It does not beat regionalisation

Median daily Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE; 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than the river’s own mean). FlowCast never reads the river’s flow. The classical ungauged method borrows calibrated GR4J parameters from nearby dev gauges and does better overall; the gauged and operational rows read information FlowCast does not have.

Reading the gauge: a nowcast

A fine-tuned copy also reads the river’s observed flow up to lead days before each day. It beats persistence at every lead, but it is a gauged method: compare it with the gauged GR4J (0.473 / 0.517) and with NWM (0.550 / 0.687), which reads no gauge at run time but is calibrated on many of these gauges.

The ratchet · 800 frozen simulated basins per physics version · 52 real dev basins (WY2001–14)

Eight rungs, two kept

A rung is kept if it beats the incumbent by more than 0.01 median NSE on the frozen simulated set of its own physics version and the dev median NSE drops by no more than 5 %. The loss fix (r0b) and the dev-measured water-yield prior (r1b) passed; flashier runoff improved peaks but cost dev NSE twice.

The loss bias

Weigh basins by what the network can see

The usual NSE* loss divides each basin’s error by the spread of its own flow. In simulation that spread depends on hidden parameters, so wet, flashy draws were down-weighted and the network learned to run low (dev high-flow bias −51 %). Scaling by the Fu runoff ratio of the forcing, which the network can see, fixed it: dev KGE 0.32 → 0.44, high-flow bias −27 %.

The water yield

ERA5-Land misses rain in the mountains

On each dev basin the simulator was run with ERA5-Land rain × m, and m was read where the simulated mean flow matched the gauge. The fit, log m = −0.088 + 0.058 elev(km) + 0.153 snow share + 0.397 log(P/3 mm), runs from 0.53 on the dry plains to 1.71 on the Olympic Peninsula. The simulator’s true rain became ERA5 × m; the network still reads ERA5. It is a regional average: South Prairie Creek (WA), in the river above, comes out 1.76× too wet.

Read this before comparing

Every method here, FlowCast included, is driven by ERA5-Land through Caravan. ERA5-Land under-catches mountain and Pacific Northwest precipitation (50 basins have a runoff ratio above 1) and drifts about 6 % between decades. That caps every score on this page. Published LSTMs driven by Daymet or NLDAS reach median NSE of about 0.69–0.76 on CAMELS; those numbers are not comparable with these.

The three rivers were drawn by a fixed seed within snow, arid and humid strata of the exam basins; none was used for training or for choosing anything. The ungauged network never reads their flow. Rule notes: the four water-yield coefficients were fitted on the same 52 dev basins that gate the rungs (partly in-sample); the climate statics are computed from the forcing of the window being predicted (exam-period forcing, no flow).
