StormNet / Space weather

How deep will the storm go?

A geomagnetic storm weakens the magnetic field near Earth’s equator. Pick a real storm, from the May 2024 superstorm to a quiet week, and play it hour by hour: every forecast, StormNet’s and three physics models’, is recomputed in your browser. Push the solar wind south, speed it up or squeeze it, and watch the ring current respond.

The honest part

What the network adds

Very little, so far. StormNet is a physics forecast plus a learned correction trained only on simulated storms. On real storms the correction neither helps nor hurts: StormNet and the tuned two-state physics are level on both exams. A dev check that gave the physics the actual next three hours of wind, which no forecast could have, cut its error only from 4.36 to 4.21 nT. The remaining miss is in the physics and in the index’s own hourly noise.

Tried before opening the reserve

Three attempts, all rejected on dev

Assimilating the ring-current split from the past day of observed Dst, pressure-dependent injection (Wang, Chao & Lin 2003), and a four-times-larger readout reading that past Dst (seed mean 0.1325 against 0.1340) all failed the rule fixed beforehand. Only the error bars changed. Every attempt is in the build notebook.

The simulator

Two recovery times

Southward wind fields inject energy into the ring current. Fast and slow components carry that history forward. Solar-wind pressure also changes the magnetic field measured at Earth. The network learns a correction to this physical forecast from simulated Dst.

The ratchet

Four rungs, two physics versions

Adding wind history failed on the first simulator and passed when all candidates were rescored on the two-state physics. The calibrated R3 cleared the margin and all three real dev years. Mixing in the old simulator was rejected.

Research replay using Earth-arrival-aligned solar wind and historical Dst; 2022–2025 Dst is Kyoto’s provisional or quicklook index. The 2022–2025 reserve was opened once, after every v1.0.1 decision was committed. The six replayed storms were chosen by observed depth alone: the deepest storm of each reserve year, the deepest primary-exam storm and one quiet block drawn by seed.
