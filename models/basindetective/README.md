# BasinDetective <sub>v1.0</sub>

> **How a river's flow changes in a decade it has not seen, from its own gauge record**

> [!WARNING]
> Research model. On the change between decades it is level with a per-basin calibrated GR4J model on five of six signatures and behind it on mean flow; its reservoir detector is at chance on real basins (AUC 0.49), and its four hidden properties have no real truth (graded on simulated twins only).

*Given nine years of a river basin's weather and flow, how do its flow signatures change in another decade, is a reservoir upstream, and what are its hidden stores?*

BasinDetective reads a river basin's daily precipitation, temperature and potential evaporation and its gauged streamflow for about nine years, plus the weather of another decade. It says how six flow signatures change in that other decade (mean flow, runoff ratio, baseflow index, low flow Q5, high flow Q95, half-flow date), whether a reservoir holding more than 10 % of the annual flow sits upstream, and four hidden properties (slow-store time constant, groundwater share, soil capacity, precipitation multiplier), each with an error bar. It was trained only on 20,000 WaterSim basins driven by real ERA5-Land weather from non-exam years, and examined once on 619 real CAMELS-US basins, predicting 1989-99 from 1999-2008. With paired intervals it ties calibrated GR4J on five of the six changes and loses on mean flow (rho 0.76 vs 0.79), needs no per-basin calibration, and its bars are too wide; regulation detection did not transfer.

## Run it

```bash
cd models/basindetective
pip install numpy scipy pandas
python demo.py        # about 2 s
```

or from anywhere in the repo: `mlratchet demo basindetective`.

```python
import pandas as pd
import basindetective as bd
det = bd.load()                                   # weights/basindetective_v1.npz, 5-member ensemble
cal = pd.read_csv('examples/camels_14020000_cal.csv', index_col=0, parse_dates=True)
tgt = pd.read_csv('examples/camels_14020000_tgt.csv', index_col=0, parse_dates=True)
res = bd.predict(det, cal[['P', 'T_mean', 'PET']], cal['Q_mm'].values, tgt)
res['changes']['d_logq']                          # {'mean', 'sd', 'unit'}: log ratio of target-decade mean flow
res['target_period']['q_mean']                    # calibration value, prediction, 68 % range (mm/day)
res['regulation']['p_regulated'], res['hidden']['slow_store_days']
```

## The exam

619 CAMELS-US exam basins, gauged protocol: the network reads 1999-2008 forcing and flow and 1989-99 forcing and predicts the change of six signatures; truth is each basin's own 1989-99 record. Run once (training/exam.json). The fairest classical baseline is GR4J+snow calibrated per basin on the same 1999-2008 flow (WaterData), scored as its model-implied change; also GR4J's simulated 1989-99 minus the observed 1999-2008 value, Budyko-Fu with w fitted on 1999-2008, and no change. No NWM, regionalisation or dev-tuned baseline exists for the change task (NWM was not fetched). Intervals: seen re-score of 18 Nov 2026 (training/rescore.py), which reproduces exam.json; paired bootstrap over the 619 basins (10,000 resamples, seed 20261118) of the model minus each baseline, per-basin values in training/exam_per_basin.csv, summary in training/exam_rescore.json. Verdict: loss if any baseline is better with an interval excluding 0, win only if every baseline is beaten that way, else tie. The unpaired Fisher-z / Zou intervals of the import stay in each note as a cross-check. Result: on the six changes, a loss on mean flow (to GR4J model-implied, -0.036 [-0.063, -0.008]) and five ties; all six median-error rows tie; levels: wins on mean flow, runoff ratio, Q5 and Q95, ties on BFI and half-flow date; regulation at chance on all three HydroATLAS truths; against USGS peak codes 5/6 (11 positives) above chance, AUC 0.711 [0.543, 0.863], but level with the classical scores. Every number here is on real data that never chose anything.

### Where it wins

- Needs no per-basin calibration and stays level with a GR4J model calibrated on each basin's own 1999-2008 flow on five of six changes (paired intervals include 0), and gives an error bar with every answer.
- Beats 'no change' in median error, paired intervals excluding 0, on mean flow (0.083 vs 0.184), runoff ratio (0.030 vs 0.038), Q95 (0.108 vs 0.204) and half-flow date (6.0 vs 8.3 days); beats calibrated GR4J's median error on Q5 (0.126 vs 0.163).
- Levels of the 1989-99 signatures: wins against both the basin's own 1999-2008 value and GR4J on mean flow, runoff ratio, Q5 and Q95 (paired intervals, but by only +0.004 to +0.007 Spearman: practically equal, since the own record alone scores 0.97-0.98); far ahead of GR4J on BFI (0.979 vs 0.787) and Q5 (0.975 vs 0.778).
- Error bars are never too narrow on the changes: 95 % bars cover 95-99 % of exam basins.

### Where it loses

- Mean-flow change: rho 0.757 against 0.793 for calibrated GR4J's model-implied change, paired difference -0.036 [-0.063, -0.008]: a loss, which the unpaired import interval had called a tie.
- Regulation does not transfer: AUC 0.485 [0.395, 0.574] against HydroATLAS storage > 10 % (37 positives), indistinguishable from chance (0.5), against 0.91 on simulated basins; 92 false alarms for 5 hits at p > 0.5. Storage > 50 % (0.53) and any reservoir (0.44) are at chance too. Against USGS peak codes 5/6 (11 positives) it is above chance, AUC 0.711 [0.543, 0.863], but not better than GR4J misfit or seasonal damping.
- On BFI and Q5 changes its median error is no better than 'no change' (0.0185 vs 0.0181; 0.126 vs 0.129; paired intervals include 0).
- The 68 % bars are too wide: 73-83 % of exam truths fall inside, and every coverage interval lies above 68 %. The widen-only factors were fitted on the 1980s dev window, which drifted more than the exam decade.
- Where ERA5-Land undercatches (gauged runoff ratio > 1, 42 basins), mean-flow change rho 0.22 (GR4J 0.21).
- On the BFI and half-flow-date levels it adds nothing measurable over its own record (BFI) or GR4J (half-flow date).

## Limits

- Weather data caps every method: the forcing is ERA5-Land, which under-catches mountain and Pacific Northwest precipitation (50 of the 671 CAMELS-US basins have a 1989-99 runoff ratio above 1, 44 in 1999-2008; 42 of the 619 exam basins in the gauged decade) and drifts about 6 % between decades (more precipitation in the 1980s). Literature numbers from Daymet- or NLDAS-forced LSTMs (NSE about 0.69-0.76) are not comparable.
- Gauged mode only: it needs about nine water years of the basin's own daily flow. Trained on 9 gauged and 10 target water years of CAMELS-US / ERA5-Land; other regions, record lengths and forcing products are extrapolation.
- The decadal drift (v3) was measured on dev twins for 1980-89 vs 1999-2008 and scaled linearly to the 9.5-year exam separation; it is one number for all regions.
- Simulated reservoirs follow generic operating policies; CAMELS-US basins are chosen to be minimally impacted, and HydroATLAS upstream volumes can be wrong where a pour point snaps to a larger river. The log-storage sigma is not calibrated.
- Hidden properties have no real truth in this data package; 'slow store' means a different store in each model structure (HBV lower box, GR4J routing store linearised, SAC-SMA lower primary, VIC-lite ARNO baseflow), and the precipitation-multiplier twins all have m = 1.
- Rule notes: dev data were used both to fit physics and to gate rungs (the decadal-drift mean was fitted on the dev basins and gated on the same dev basins: partly in-sample). Free-window forcing from exam basins (1951-1988) was used in the bank, as the brief allows; no exam Q was used for training. The exam was untouched.

## Physics

WaterSim, the shared water platform's simulator (ML Ratchet water platform, Phase 1): four conceptual runoff models (HBV, GR4J, SAC-SMA, VIC-lite), degree-day and energy-balance snow on elevation bands, unit-hydrograph routing, reservoirs with operating policies (SOP, rule curve, Hanasaki) and irrigation, driven by real ERA5-Land forcing from non-exam years or by its stochastic weather generator. BasinDetective's bank generator (training/simbank.py, no edit to the shared package) separates the TRUE forcing that drives the simulator from the SEEN forcing the network reads, and adds a gauge model; physics v2-v4 calibrate both on the dev basins.

**Simulator**

- Bank: 10,000 basins per physics version (shards 0-39) and a frozen set of 1,000 (shards 900-903). 40 % camels prior (dev-basin attributes, jittered) with the donor's own ERA5-Land forcing 1951-1988; 35 % default prior on a random CAMELS basin's 1951-1988 forcing; 25 % stochastic weather. Layout: 1 spin-up year, 10-year target decade (forcing only), 9 gauged years. Random model (4) and snow routine (2) per basin; target-decade climate perturbed in half the basins (P × U(0.88, 1.12), T + U(-1, 1) °C, PET × (1 + 0.025 dT))
- SEEN vs TRUE forcing: persistent precipitation multiplier, daily error cv 0.25, snow undercatch U(0, 0.1), temperature offset N(0, 0.7 °C); v2: log multiplier ~ N(-0.076 + 0.30 frac_snow, 0.116), fitted on dev (calib_pbias.py); v3: decadal drift, TRUE target-decade P = SEEN / exp(delta), delta ~ N(0.029, 0.038), fitted on dev twins (calib_drift.py); v4: year-to-year error TRUE P × exp(e_y), e_y ~ N(0, 0.10) (calib_annual.py)
- Gauge model: v1 WaterSim default (AR(1) rho 0.9, cv 0.08, rating revisions, gaps); v2+ fitted on dev twins: AR(1) rho 0.999, cv 0.02, 3 significant figures in cfs (dynamics-feature gap score 5.84 → 4.63, calib_gauge.json)
- Labels: six signature changes computed with an exact port of WaterData's signature code; slow-store constant, groundwater share and soil capacity from each model's parameters; true / seen precipitation; reservoir storage / natural annual flow

**Checked against independent references**

- WaterSim Phase 1 (shared/water/checks): GR4J vs airGR (independent Fortran in R) 1.0e-7 mm on 4,017 days; HBV snow and soil states vs TUWmodel ≤ 1e-12 mm on 12,053 days (runoff routing differs by design: NSE 0.999); water-balance closure 1.4e-15 relative on 121 runs
- Signature port features.sigs vs waterdata.signatures: max relative difference 1.3e-6 on 52 dev basins (as shipped; needs the Caravan data to re-run)
- Twin test on 52 dev basins × 5 dev-calibrated setups (260 twins, training/twin_final.json): change rho on twins 0.33-0.85 vs on real 0.25-0.61; the twins' own change ranks the real change at rho 0.27-0.69 (simulator skill); p_regulated median 0.06 on twins vs 0.21 on real (0.18 on unregulated real basins)
- Reproduction (18 Nov 2026, training/rescore.py, seen): the released weights through the shipped exam path (realfeats.py, exam.py) on the Caravan time series reproduce every number in exam.json to 1e-9 relative, except the two Budyko-Fu R2 values (1e-7); the NWIS peak-code row, re-scored after the NWIS fetch, reproduces too; WaterData's GR4J daily simulations were re-made from the shipped parameters as run_gr4j.py writes them
- Import check (18 Nov 2026, training/import_check.py): every GR4J-vs-own-record and no-change number in exam.json, the level baselines and the four examples' truth re-derive exactly from WaterData's shipped signatures.parquet
- numpy == torch (float32) < 6e-6 and the shipped page's JS == Python to 2e-7 sigma (as shipped, not re-run). The site instrument re-computes the 150 features and the ensemble in TypeScript: equal to the Python package to 3.5e-15 on 36 cases (4 basins × 9 what-ifs, float16 weights); float16 weights move the outputs by ≤ 3.7e-4 relative
