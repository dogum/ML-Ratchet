# FaultFinder <sub>v1.1</sub>

> **Is this air handler faulty today, and which fault is it, from one day of trend log?**

> [!WARNING]
> Research model: it flags days for a person to look at and does not replace commissioning. At P = 0.5 it raises a false alarm on a third of healthy air-handler days, and v1.1 raises false alarms on days with a reference (seen exam: AHU 33 → 56 %). Its fault diagnosis is no better than naming the commonest fault.

*From one day of an air handler's or rooftop unit's building-automation trend log, how likely is a fault, and which fault is it?*

FaultFinder reads one local day of an air handler's (AHU) or rooftop unit's (RTU) building-automation trend log (outdoor, return, mixed, supply and zone air temperatures, the supply-air setpoint, damper and valve commands, fan speed, power and status, occupancy; 1- to 15-min samples, any subset) and returns P(fault), a probability over 18 fault classes, a severity for the top class and the day's extra HVAC energy. It learned only from BuildingSim simulations: 38,987 simulated days of random RTU and VAV buildings in 17 US climates, with real sensor noise and quirks from 20 real dev days. It was examined once on 217 real lab and building days from four sources (Iowa Energy Resource Station, LBNL FLEXLAB, ORNL's rooftop unit and Drexel's Nesbitt Hall), and re-scored on 17 Nov 2026 against rules tuned on the same dev days, with 95 % intervals. v1.1 (17 Nov 2026) can also compare the day with the same unit's last 1-3 logged days: v1.0's network plus 47 reference input rows, trained on simulated days with v1.0's weights frozen. Without reference days it gives v1.0's log-odds. On real days with a reference it shifts the log-odds up by about 0.1 to 0.7, which raised false alarms on the (seen) exam.

Twenty fault types are injected into the plant and logged as a BMS-style trend. ## Run it

```bash
cd models/faultfinder
pip install numpy pandas
python demo.py        # about 1 s
```

or from anywhere in the repo: `mlratchet demo faultfinder`.

```python
import pandas as pd
from faultfinder import FaultFinder
ff = FaultFinder()
df = pd.read_csv("examples/nesbitt_NES_20180711_AHU-1.csv")   # contract column names + 'time_local'
r = ff.read_day(df)                                            # single-day mode (v1.0's log-odds)
refs = [pd.read_csv(f"examples/references/NES_201807{d}_AHU-1.csv") for d in ("09", "10")]
r2 = ff.read_day(df, ref_days=refs)                            # v1.1: compared with the unit's earlier days
print(r["p_fault"], r2["p_fault"], r2["diagnosis"][:3])
```

### Where it wins

- Catching faults at P = 0.5 (seen, v1.1): 86 % of faulty AHU days against 52 % for the APAR rules with dev-tuned tolerances (+34 points, 95 % CI [+22, +46]); at Nesbitt Hall, a real occupied building, 88 % against 62 % for a dev-fitted mixing-box residual (+27, [+10, +44]). The price is the false alarms below.
- Ranking: FLEXLAB AUC 0.97 against 0.74 (+0.23, CI [+0.02, +0.48], 23 days). AHU AUC 0.78 against 0.69 for the dev-tuned rules (+0.09, CI [−0.00, +0.17]) and Nesbitt 0.79 against 0.71 (+0.08, CI [−0.03, +0.20]) are ahead but not established.

### Where it loses

- False alarms: at P = 0.5 v1.1 flags 56 % of healthy AHU days (v1.0 33 %; Iowa ERS 67 %, Nesbitt 44 %) against 2 % for the textbook-tolerance rules (+54 points, CI [+44, +64]). On real days with a reference the reference rows add about +0.1 to +0.7 to the log-odds, healthy days included (ERS healthy +0.71 on average).
- Balanced accuracy is no better than rules tuned on the same 20 dev days: AHU 0.65 against 0.68 (CI [−0.11, +0.06]), all units 0.64 against 0.69 (CI [−0.13, +0.03]); Iowa ERS 0.55, a loss (−0.14, CI [−0.26, −0.02]). v1.0: AHU 0.70, ERS 0.65.
- At Nesbitt Hall the textbook rules were a weak baseline (AUC 0.54): a mixing-box residual fitted on the dev days reaches 0.71 and the dev-tuned rules stay at 0.54; most of v1.0's Nesbitt margin was against the weak one.
- Rooftop unit (17 days): AUC 0.72, the same as the one-line rule 'fan on while unoccupied' (v1.0 0.65); balanced accuracy 0.53, a tie with the rules (v1.0 0.47, a loss to the majority guess).
- Diagnosis: top-1 18 % against 21 % for always naming the commonest fault, top-3 50 % against 55 % for the three commonest. Ties by the interval rule: no evidence of skill beyond the commonest class.
- At strict false-alarm rates the rules catch more on the point values: 32 % against 29 % at 2 %, 41 % against 30 % at 5 % (AHU, report-only thresholds; the intervals span zero).

## Limits

- Diagnosis on real data is about as good as guessing the commonest fault. Use the top three as a checklist, not an answer; outdoor-air damper stuck is top-1 on 1 of 14 days (v1.0 0).
- v1.1 raises false alarms on days with a reference (seen exam: AHU 33 → 56 %, Iowa ERS 27 → 67 %). The reference rows were trained on simulated days only; on real days they add a near-constant +0.1 to +0.7 to the log-odds, a sim-to-real offset. The dev gate could not see it: only 2 of the 20 dev days have a reference. Leave the reference days out to get v1.0's behaviour.
- Probabilities are squashed toward 0.5 by a temperature fitted on 20 days (T = 4.2; v1.0 4.3): 157 of 217 exam days fall in 0.4-0.6, and two of the four false alarms on the dev days sit at P = 0.501. v1.0's uncalibrated Brier score was lower (0.204 vs 0.216); for v1.1 the calibrated one is (0.220 vs 0.225); both intervals span zero. A two-parameter (Platt) map fitted on the dev days (r7b, not kept) cut dev false alarms from 4 to 1 of 11 leave-one-out but also cut detections from 6 to 4 of 9, and its leave-one-out Brier score was worse (0.258 vs 0.246).
- History is short: v1.1 compares a day with at most the unit's last 3 days within a week, and only on simulated evidence. That most false alarms are unit habits is a hypothesis, not a measurement: on the 11 healthy dev days the nearest day in feature space was the same unit's for 4 of 10, and subtracting the unit's other healthy days' log-odds raised dev false alarms from 3 to 4 of 8. A multi-day rung (r7_context, 17 Nov 2026) was tried and not kept: on simulated days with a reference it scored 0.563 against 0.537 with the reference removed, but its 3-seed mean skill on frozen v5 was 0.558 against v1.0's 0.561, and only 2 of the 20 dev days have an earlier same-unit day within 7 days. r8 (v1.1) kept v1.0 frozen and added only the reference rows: frozen v5 skill 0.572 against 0.561.
- Single-zone simulator with ideal controls and no exhaust damper, return fan, duct model, chiller-off or chilled-water sensor faults: 13 of the exam's 27 fault types cannot be diagnosed at all.
- Small exam: 217 days from five facilities. The 95 % interval on AHU AUC minus the best baseline is about ±0.09, on the RTU ±0.4. The fixed-false-alarm recall rows set their thresholds on the exam's own healthy days and are report-only.
- The energy-waste output is not validated (Spearman 0.23 on 46 paired ERS days; v1.0 0.19).

## Physics

BuildingSim, the collection's building simulator (shared building platform, Phase 1): a 5R1C zone heat balance with solar gains, wind and stack infiltration and moisture, and air handlers built from fan laws, a mixing box, cooling and heating coils, chillers and boilers and DX curves from DOE reference data. Twenty fault types are injected into the plant and logged as a BMS-style trend. A FaultFinder wrapper (training/simbank.py, noise.py) makes the log look like a real BMS: commands rather than actuator positions, an installed damper characteristic, larger static sensor offsets and operator start/stop jitter.

**Simulator**

- Fault bank: random BuildingSim buildings with packaged RTUs, VAV air handlers with chiller/boiler or DX, or VAV on district energy, in 17 TMYx US cities, any season. Each 12-day window is simulated healthy and with one fault from day 5 on; every local day is a sample (faulted days with a visible effect, plus healthy days). Two banks of 38,987 days: v1 (generic controls) and v3 (dev-calibrated).
- Twenty BuildingSim fault types map onto 18 classes: stuck and leaking outdoor-air dampers, economizer faults, stuck and leaking cooling and heating valves, reheat valve leak, cooling capacity (coil and condenser fouling, refrigerant charge), low airflow, sensor biases (supply, outdoor, mixed, return air), zone setpoint or zone sensor offset, schedule, VAV box stuck, chiller or boiler efficiency.
- Of the exam taxonomy's 27 fault types, 14 map onto a class. The other 13 (exhaust damper, supply duct leak, heating-coil fouling and capacity, return fan, unstable control, reverse-acting valve, chilled-water sensors, chiller off, lighting schedule, infiltration) cannot be simulated and are graded for detection only.
- Dev calibration (bank v3, noise v4) from twin tests on the 20 dev days: the BMS logs controller commands, not positions (FLEXLAB's heating valve stuck at 100 % logs 0 %); a real damper at 100 % command mixes only 81-90 % outdoor air and Nesbitt's at 15 % mixes 14-28 %, so the logged damper is a + (b − a)·cmd^g with a ~ U(0, 0.15), b ~ U(0.75, 1), g ~ U(0.6, 1.6); mixed-air stratification sd 0.8 K and zone vs return sd 0.9 K; operator jitter of 2-3 bins and a 1-3 h off block on 15 % of days; schedule-shift faults; 25 % of units run 24×7.

**Checked against independent references**

- RC step response within 0.003 K of the closed form; steady conduction to 2e-9 relative error; energy ledger closes to 1e-8 (BuildingSim checks)
- 31 of 36 BESTEST (ASHRAE 140) results inside the 1995 reference ranges (the transcribed ranges; the official ASHRAE 140 tables are still wanted)
- Psychrometrics within 0.02 K (p95) of CoolProp; PMV within 0.003 of pythermalcomfort
- 26 of 26 BuildingSim fault signatures move the trend in the expected direction
- Numpy inference matches torch to 3.8e-6 for v1.1 (faultfinder_weights.npz.check.json; v1.0 9.5e-7); without reference days v1.1 matches v1.0's logits to 1.9e-6. The lab page's JS port (v1.0) matches numpy to 5e-6 on all 217 exam days (top class agrees on 217 of 217)
