ReservoirPilot / Hydrology

How much should the dam release today?

Pick one of six real US dams from the exam and watch a ten-year decade operated four ways: by ReservoirPilot, by SOP, by a rule tuned on the same simulator, and by a dynamic program that knew all the future inflow. Make the decade drier, add a flood, shrink the reservoir, blur the gauge or raise demand. Every policy re-runs in your browser.

The six dams are a fixed random draw within main-use strata (flood control, irrigation, hydropower; two each), not picked by result. Storage is shown as a share of capacity; release and inflow as a share of Qmax, the 99th-percentile release of the calibration decade. On the recorded decade the live pilot reproduces the exam run of the released weights to 3e-6 relative in J (float32 weights; the excerpts store inflow to 6 significant digits).

WaterData's frozen exam · 244 ResOpsUS dams + 15 CDEC reservoirs · run once

The honest part

What J does not charge for

J = mean daily (shortage ÷ demand)² + 10 (excess over Qmax ÷ Qmax)². Stored water is free. The pilot learned that: it keeps a median 38 % of capacity where SOP keeps 59 % and the DP bound 68 %, it releases a little less than demand on two days in three, and it goes over Qmax on more days than anyone (2.9 a year) but by only 4 % of Qmax. The shipped card says it ends the decade at least 20 % of capacity lower than it started on a third of the dams; that per-dam number could not be re-checked from the shipped files. A storage-aware objective is the first next rung.

Where it fails

Drier decades, big supply dams

It loses to SOP on 37 of 244 dams. By the shipped card these are mostly large irrigation and supply reservoirs, two years of inflow deep, whose scored decade was drier than the decade their demand and inflow normal came from. Try it above: turn the inflow down to ×0.7 and watch the pilot keep releasing for a normal that no longer arrives. On the 15 recent California reservoirs it only ties the tuned rule (better on 8 of 15).

The ratchet · frozen simulated sets g1 → g4 · 15 real dev dams as the gate

Ten rungs, two void runs, one re-scoring

Generator rungs (the physics axis): g1 WaterSim inflow and a random simulated historical operator; g2 adds back-calculated inflow noise (e_t − e_t−1)/K, e ~ N(0, σ·cap), which gives negative-inflow days as in ResOpsUS (dev and aux median 2 %, up to 35 %); g3 adds an upstream regulating reservoir on 30 % of tasks and gross data spikes on 10 %; g4 makes the halves non-stationary (scored-decade inflow × exp(N(0, 0.3))). g2-g4 ranges were set from the 15 dev dams and the aux dams with complete records (6 complete, inflow ratios on 29); the exam was never read. When the frozen set switched from g1 to g4, every rung was re-scored and every decision re-taken in order; the decisions below are the re-taken ones, with the g1 decision in the last column. r3 and r4 were kept on g1 and are rejected on g4, so r5 (r4 fine-tuned on g4) was judged against r2. Not rungs, logged in training/ratchet.json notes: a first objective with a hard end-storage penalty (1000 × deficit / cap) inside one-year windows punished natural seasonal drawdown and was replaced by a differentiable 365-day SOP tail; two first DPO runs (free-form head, then rule head) never applied the optimiser step (train.py lacked opt.step()), so their weights never moved; they were removed and re-run (the rows void-1 and void-2 below). The DPO free-form rung r3 was first run as "r1" (runs/r1_dpo_hd_g1; the twin test file keeps that name). r5's margin over r2 (+0.011) is barely above the 0.01 rule. Every rung, the final one included, ends the simulated windows with less water than it started with (median end − start on frozen g4: −0.23 of capacity for r8) and the ratchet never charged for it, because J does not.

The six dams come from ResOpsUS v2 (Steyaert, Condon, Turner & Voisin 2022, Scientific Data 9:34; CC BY 4.0) with GRanD attributes (Lehner et al. 2011; CC BY 4.0, via ResOpsUS+CARS). The recent exam uses California DWR's CDEC. Training inflows came from WaterSim driven by ERA5-Land forcing via Caravan (Kratzert et al. 2023; Muñoz-Sabater et al. 2021; CC BY 4.0 and Copernicus licence), which under-catches mountain and Pacific Northwest precipitation; the exam runs on recorded inflow. J and the baselines are WaterData's (Watershed Phase 1). Research benchmark, not an operating tool.
