# FireSize <sub>v1.0</sub>

> **Escape probability and final size of a new US wildfire, from its ignition record**

> [!WARNING]
> Its escape probabilities are about twice too high (mean 0.055 against a real 0.027), so read them as a ranking, not as calibrated chances. Research model: not for dispatch or resource decisions.

*Will this new fire escape to 100 acres or more, and how big will it get?*

FireSize reads a US wildfire ignition record (place, date, cause, reporting agency, discovery hour), a 250 m LANDFIRE summary around the point and gridMET weather on the discovery day and the day before. It returns P(escape to ≥ 100 acres), P(≥ 1,000 acres) and the final-size distribution over 12 FPA-FOD size classes with a median and an 80 % range. It is an ensemble of three small MLPs (83,364 weights) trained only on 8,500 simulated ignitions: FireSim free-burn spread in real 90 m LANDFIRE windows under real gridMET weather, then a containment race whose parameters were fitted on 911 real dev ignitions. Graded once on 655,869 real FPA-FOD exam ignitions (2012-2020), it ranks escapes better than climatology (AUC 0.727 vs 0.688) and fire-danger indices (0.634) but not as well as a gradient-boosting model fitted on real dev labels (0.777). It ranks fires that reach 1,000 acres better than every baseline (AUC 0.815 vs 0.803), and beats the GBM in California, the Northwest and the Rocky Mountain region. Its probabilities are about twice too high and its size point estimate is worse than climatology.

### Where it wins

- Large fires: the best of every method at ranking which ignitions reach 1,000 acres (AUC 0.815 against 0.803 for the GBM fitted on real dev labels, 0.767 for climatology), in both 2012-19 and 2020.
- Escape ranking beats climatology (+0.039 AUC, 95 % bootstrap +0.035 to +0.044) and the fire-danger indices (+0.093) without ever training on a real fire outcome.
- In California (0.739 vs 0.690), the Northwest (0.776 vs 0.735) and the Rocky Mountain region (0.748 vs 0.703) it ranks escapes better than the GBM fitted on real labels, and better than every other baseline there.

### Where it loses

- Escape ranking overall: AUC 0.727 against 0.777 for the GBM fitted on dev real labels (−0.050, 95 % −0.053 to −0.047); 0.740 against 0.789 in 2020.
- Southeast (AUC 0.566 against 0.704) and Southern Plains (0.715 against 0.808), the two biggest gaps; also behind the GBM in the Northern Rockies, Great Basin, Southwest and Northeast/Midwest.
- Calibration: mean P(escape) 0.055 against a real rate of 0.027, so its Brier score (0.0273) is worse than predicting the prevalence (0.0260).
- Size point estimate: median |log10 error| 0.642 against 0.505 for climatology and 0.510 for the GBM, with a +0.22 log10 bias; it under-sizes the big MTBS fires by 3.3 log10.

## Limits

- Escape probabilities are about twice too high. The widen-only temperature was fitted to the size-class likelihood, not to escape, and raised the dev-gate Brier score from 0.0274 to 0.0285 before the exam; the release kept it as frozen.
- Suppression is an effective race model fitted on dev twins, not a measured one: 10 of its 31 fitted parameters sit at a bound (response 0.1 h, line rates 30 times the handbook values, a federal line-rate term of e^−3, a confine-only share of 0.5, ROS spread 0.3, and five more; ratchet.yaml lists them). No validated suppression data (ICS-209) were used.
- The simulator has no spotting, no re-burn after containment and no weather after 96 h; daily gridMET is disaggregated to hours. The largest fires are under-sized as a result.
- Southeast and Southern Plains: debris, prescribed and agricultural burning, small parcels and dense fast suppression are not in the simulator. LANDFIRE 2016 fuels are stale for 2017-2020 and scarred by 2012-16 fires.
- Landscape inputs are 250 m national grids (a coarse summary by design; the simulator ran on 90 m windows). Labels are FPA-FOD reported sizes, rounded to 0.01, 0.1 or 1 acre in most records; complexes and merged fires are booked to one record.
- Twin-test ceiling: the network matches the simulator (AUC 0.906 against the simulated twin's outcome), but the simulator matches reality at 0.671. Better networks will not help much; better physics and suppression would.
- Rule notes: FireSize read FPA-FOD records straight from the platform's parquet, filtered by its split (common.fod_records), because firedata has no FPA-FOD accessor. It loads the whole 2012-2020 table, then filters; in the shipped scripts only exam.py and, after the exam, post_exam.py and build_release.py select the exam records, and every other script asks for 'dev'. The dev calib half fitted the suppression model, the importance weights and the temperature; the gate half is out of sample. r7 passed the gate AUC test by 0.0004, and no interval was computed for any rung decision.
- CONUS, 2012-2020 only (FPA-FOD has no later records); a 2021 or later ignition is outside both the training weather years and the exam.
- Slash-blowdown ignitions (audit, 25 Nov 2026): no simulated ignition landed on LANDFIRE codes 201-204, so the point-fuel input pt_SB has sd 1e-6 and a real one enters at z = 1e6. Escape probability is then exactly 0 and the median 1.73 acres whatever the weather. 91 of the 655,869 exam ignitions were scored that way (exact zeros in exam_preds). Since 27 Nov 2026 probs() warns on inputs beyond 8 sd; FireSize.z_clip = 8 clips them (not the released behaviour).

## The ratchet

**Objective:** Ranked probability skill score (RPSS over the 12 FPA-FOD size classes, against the frozen set's own importance-weighted class climatology) of the network's distribution against each simulated ignition's soft label (its reported-size distribution over 24 suppression draws), on the frozen simulated validation ignitions (304 frozen windows). One frozen set per physics × suppression version: v0|lit, v0|v0acc (743 ignitions each), v1|v1acc, v1|v1reg, v1|v1rep, v1|v1rep2 (1,175 each). Decisions use the latest, v1|v1rep2.  
**Rule:** Keep if the sim RPSS on the latest frozen set beats the incumbent's on that same set by more than 0.01 AND the dev-gate escape AUC is at least the incumbent's − 0.01 AND the dev-gate size MAE (log10) is at most 1.05 × the incumbent's. Every rung was re-scored on every frozen set and the decisions re-taken in order on v1|v1rep2.

## Physics

FireSim, the shared fire platform's simulator (shared/fire/firesim): Rothermel/Albini surface fire on the 40 Scott & Burgan fuel models, Van Wagner crown fire, minimum-travel-time spread on the real 90 m LANDFIRE window, Nelson dead fuel moisture from hourly weather disaggregated from gridMET, live moisture from the GSI; no spotting. Fires smaller than one 90 m cell (0.81 ha) are tracked as an analytic sub-cell ellipse, because most FPA-FOD fires are. Each simulated fire burns freely for 96 h; a Fried & Fried (1996) containment race then sets its final size: line building starts after a response time and escalates, and the fire is contained when the line built catches the line needed (a share of the ellipse perimeter). FireSize wraps FireSim without editing it, with two physics versions: v0 as shipped and v1 (agriculture, LANDFIRE 93, burns as GR1; an ignition on a non-burnable cell moves to the nearest burnable cell within 180 m).

**Simulator**

- Ignition prior (prior.py): 2,400 windows of 18 km centred on dev ignition locations with N(0, 20 km) jitter, 8 ignitions each; each ignition borrows the cause, agency, discovery hour and date (± 7 days) of a dev ignition within 150 km. Every window passes the platform's check_landscape and every weather period its check_weather, so no exam footprint or exam fire-day weather is used. 304 windows are frozen for validation
- Free burn (sim.py): pre-discovery burn time ~ LN(0, 1) h and a ROS multiplier ~ logU[0.2, 1.5] per simulation, both reweighted to the calibrated distributions by importance weights; about 1 s per simulation. The v1 bank holds 8,500 training ignitions plus the twins (18 MB)
- Suppression and reporting (supp.py, calib.py): two attack modes (a share of fires only confined or monitored, at 2 % of the line rate), access, slope and federal line-rate terms, reporting floors (0.1 acre, regional 1-acre shares, a 0.01-acre share). 31 parameters fitted by Powell on 911 dev twins (calib half: dev ignitions simulated 4 times each in their own landscape and weather) by the likelihood of each twin's real size class plus an escape term
- Labels: each simulated ignition's reported-size distribution over 24 suppression draws (soft labels)

**Checked against independent references**

- FireSim (platform checks, shared/fire/checks): 436 of 436 published BehavePlus / GTR values within half the last digit; agreement with pyretechnics to 5e-5 (6,720 cases); uniform-fuel fires grow as the predicted ellipse (mean area ratio 0.981)
- Suppression fit (results/calibration/calib_v1rep2.json): twin size-class NLL 3.60 with literature values, 2.64 fitted; 2-fold held out 2.66 / 2.73 against 3.56 / 3.68 for the literature values. Twin escape rate 0.024 against a real 0.026
- Twin test (twin.py, 911 dev twins, results/twin_final.json): the released network predicts the escape of the simulated twin of each real ignition at AUC 0.906, but the calibrated simulator predicts the real outcome at only 0.671. The network reads a real ignition the way the simulator does; the ceiling is the physics and suppression model, not the network. The network's own AUC against the real outcome is 0.734
- Rothermel inputs: a lookup table from FireSim's behave() (ros_lut.npz, fuel code x 14 1-h moistures x 12 wind speeds). On import, recomputing the four ROS columns of the 8 demo ignitions from their 13 x 13 fuel patches reproduces the exam features to 1.1e-7
- The released weights file is byte-identical to the model the exam ran (sha256 72289217…3902 in results/exam.json); demo.py reproduces the exam's P(escape) for its 8 ignitions to 1e-4. The site instrument (TypeScript, float16 weights) matches numpy on the same weights to 1.1e-14 on 104 cases (8 ignitions x 13 what-ifs, Rothermel inputs included); float16 moves P(escape) by at most 6.4e-6 from the float64 release

## Next rungs

- Escape-aware calibration: keep T = 1 for P(escape) and widen only the size tail, or fit the temperature on the escape term. On the dev twins the unwidened escape rate already matches (0.023 vs 0.026). It is likely, but not computed, that T = 1 would also have given a better exam Brier.
- Southeast and Southern Plains physics: burnable agricultural and debris-burn fuel states by season, and a WUI or parcel suppression term fitted on dev twins in those regions.
- Containment beyond 96 h with real multi-day gridMET for fires that are not contained, to fix the −3.3 log10 under-sizing of MTBS fires.
- Model FPA-FOD's reporting granularity (0.1 and 1 acre point masses) inside the simulator's reporting floor.
- More twins per region: the guard skipped 45 % of the escaped dev twins; LANDFIRE windows that pass the guard could recover some.
- Widen the suppression-parameter bounds or change the race's form; add owner class and position through the simulator (the GBM gains from them between regions).
- Queued (audit): slash-blowdown ignitions in the simulated bank (or a floor on one-hot sds), retrained and judged on dev; a dated re-score of the 91 affected exam ignitions on the owner's approval.
