It learned batteries from physics alone. Then it read 118 real cells.

CellSense's networks never trained on a real battery. They learned from a physics model of a lithium-iron-phosphate cell (lithium moving through graphite and LFP crystals, heat, slow wear), tuned to six real cells that are left out of every test. Now CellSense reads one fast discharge of a real cell and says how much charge is really left, how much resistance it has picked up, what is wearing out, and roughly how many cycles remain.

The hidden capacity

A fast discharge leaves charge behind. CellSense finds it.

Every cycle in Severson et al.'s experiment drains the cell at 4C, four times its rated current, down to 2.0 V, then holds 2.0 V until the current fades to a trickle. The 4C part is the curve; the hold recovers charge that was there but couldn't get out fast enough. In a new cell the hold adds about 16 mAh. Near end of life it adds up to 120 mAh. CellSense sees only the 4C curve and estimates the total.

How it works

Physics in, diagnosis out.

The physics

A single-particle model of each electrode: lithium diffusing through graphite and LFP particles, reacting at their surfaces, an electrolyte that polarizes over minutes, and a cell that heats up. Wear is written as Dubarry's degradation modes plus resistance and a slowly growing share of poorly connected graphite. A whole aging trajectory to end of life adds the knee: lost graphite until the graphite limits the cell, then lithium plating.

Calibrated, then let loose

Six real cells, two from each batch, set the new-cell physics from their first ten cycles: curve shape, heating, capacity and pulse resistance. They are left out of every exam. Their worn-out curves showed what the first simulator missed: charge that only comes out slowly. Training cells are then drawn around those six and aged at random.

The network

A 1-D convolutional network reads the discharge along the voltage axis: capacity and can temperature at 250 voltages from 3.5 V to 2.0 V, next to the same cell when new and their difference. For the lifetime it also gets the cycle number and the charging time. Every output comes with its own error bar.

Use it

One Python file, two weight files.

Give it a 4C discharge of an A123 APR18650M1A-like LFP cell as capacity and temperature against voltage, optionally with the same cell's early discharge, its cycle number and its charging time. It needs only numpy.
