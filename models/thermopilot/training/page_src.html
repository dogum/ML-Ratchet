ThermoPilot

A learned thermostat for homes and small buildings. Every hour it picks a heating and a cooling setpoint so the bill is low and the rooms stay inside the occupant's comfort band. It learned when to pre-heat and pre-cool by imitating a controller that could see the future, inside a building simulator. It was graded on twins of real homes and buildings under their real weather and real tariffs.

The exam

No real thermostat logs were available (ecobee's Donate Your Data needs a research application), so the exam runs in the simulator, made as real as we could: twins of real exam units built from their real characteristics, run under their real weather and real tariffs. Neither the MPC nor ThermoPilot knows the house exactly: the simulated house is a perturbed copy (envelope ±12 %, air leakage ±25 %, thermal mass class, internal loads) of the twin the controllers are given. Nothing here was used for training or for choosing anything.

Bill = energy + demand charges + gas for the month; fixed customer charges are left out because no controller changes them. Brackets: 90 % bootstrap interval over units. Comfort: kelvin-hours outside the occupant's band per episode, mean PPD while home (ISO 7730), on/off switches of the HVAC per day. "Perfect foresight" knows the true house, the true weather and the true state and may re-simulate the month (best of the DP with a monthly peak target and the rules); it is a practical bound, not a proven optimum.

How far can the simulator be trusted?

The one place with real heat-pump data is WPuQ: 38 heat-pump homes in Lower Saxony, metered every hour. They have no building metadata, so each home got a generic heat-pump twin. This is a report-only check: nothing in ThermoPilot was tuned on it.

The ratchet

A rung is kept only if it beats the incumbent on a frozen simulated validation set (60 episodes) by more than 0.01 skill and passes a dev gate on 30 dev episodes (RECS dev respondents and BDG2 dev buildings, never in the exam). Skill = share of the possible saving captured: 0 is the fixed thermostat, 1 is perfect foresight, both on cost plus comfort penalty.

    How it was built

    Teacher

    A dynamic program over the building's thermal mass temperature (0.25 K grid, 24 h horizon, replanned every hour) that sees the true weather and the true house. It scores 36 setpoint pairs (heating off / band edge / pre-heat +0–3 K, cooling off / band edge / pre-cool −0–3 K) on energy cost, gas, demand-charge increments and λ × kelvin-hours outside the band (λ = $3 per K h per 150 m²). Its model is BuildingSim's own 5R1C step map, so it is exact up to infiltration linearisation. For demand tariffs it re-simulates the month with a peak target (BuildingSim's exact state rollback makes this possible).

    Student

    A 298→256→256→36 MLP (152k parameters, numpy at run time). Inputs: indoor temperature and its last 3 hours, its own last HVAC output, 24 h of forecast weather (with realistic forecast error), price, comfort band, heat-pump COP and capacity tables from a building card, demand-charge state. Trained on the teacher's action values (soft targets + expected regret), then on states its own mistakes lead to (DAgger). A comfort shield keeps setpoints within reach of the next 6 hours of the band.
