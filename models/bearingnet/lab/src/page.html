Physics + ML collection · rolling-bearing diagnosis

BearingNet

A crack in a ball bearing clicks every time a ball rolls over it. The click rate is fixed by the bearing's geometry and the shaft speed. BearingNet listens to 2.7 seconds of an accelerometer and says which part is damaged: outer race, inner race or a ball. It never saw a real faulty bearing while learning. It was trained only on simulated defects added into recordings of healthy machines.

The question it tests: does a model that learned from physics carry over to a machine it never heard? Short answer: see the exam below.

The physics

Four frequencies from geometry

Balls roll between a fixed outer ring and an inner ring that turns with the shaft. The cage carrying the balls turns at a bit under half the shaft speed. From the number of balls n, ball diameter d and pitch diameter D, each kind of defect has its own click rate, in multiples of the shaft speed:

Each click rings the machine like a small hammer blow, at a few kHz. So the useful trick (the classical method, too) is to band-pass the signal around those kHz resonances, take its envelope, and look at the envelope's spectrum. A fault shows up as a comb of lines at its click rate. Inner-race defects turn with the shaft, through the loaded zone and out again, so their lines get side-lines one shaft speed apart. Ball defects get side-lines at the cage speed.

How the model improved

The ratchet

Each change (a "rung") was kept only if it beat the current model by more than 0.01 on a frozen set of simulations and did not lose more than 0.03 balanced accuracy on a small real development set (10 CWRU files, 3 Paderborn bearings). When the physics changed, a new frozen set was built and every earlier rung was re-scored on it.

    Use it

    The release is bearingnet-v1.0.zip: a numpy/scipy-only module, the weights, a demo on real exam recordings, the model card and every training script.

    import bearingnet as BNet
net = BNet.BearingNet()
r = net.read(x, fs=12000, bearing='SKF6205', rpm_nominal=1750)   # or bearing=dict(n=9, d=7.94, D=39.04)
print(r['condition'], r['p'], r['rpm'], r['rpm_sig'], r['size_mm'])
