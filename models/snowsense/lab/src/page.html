SnowSense / Hydrology

How much snow is on the pillow?

A snow pillow weighs the snowpack. SnowSense never reads it. It reads only the station’s own rain gauge and thermometer, and estimates the water stored as snow every day, with error bars. Pick a real exam station and winter, then warm it, wet it, move it uphill or knock out the gauge, and watch the network and two physics models answer in your browser.

Final exam · 621 SNOTEL exam stations · water years 2011–2025 · run once

The fair fight, run after import

Physics tuned on dev

WaterSim’s default parameters are off at snow pillows: the dev calibrations put turbulent heat about five times lower and albedo ageing much faster. With the dev stations’ median parameters, frozen before scoring, the energy balance scores 0.895 on the exam and SnowSense 0.888, a tie; the tuned physics has the higher NSE at 55% of stations and gets peak snow closer (bias −4% against −9%). This is the WakeNet and StormNet lesson again: the network has learned its physics, but does not yet beat it once the physics is tuned. It does fail less often (NSE below 0.5 at 8% of stations against 10%) and is much better below 1,500 m (0.79 against 0.70).

Where it misses

Low, warm and under-caught

Peaks are 9% low (median). About half of that is the network averaging over unknown snow parameters; the rest is snow the physics does not make, such as wind drift and gauge under-catch. Stations below 1,500 m score 0.79 and the Southwest 0.78, where rain falls on thin snow. Each winter starts on 1 August with no snow knowledge, and SnowSense needs the station’s own gauge: gridded weather is untested.

The ratchet · frozen simulated sets val_p1–val_p4 · dev gate on 38 dev stations

Six rungs, one rejected

A rung is kept if it beats the incumbent by more than 0.01 median NSE on the newest frozen simulated set and the dev stations get no more than 5% worse. Every rung was re-scored on every set and the decisions re-taken on val_p4. The exam columns were filled in once, after the ladder was finished. Nothing was chosen on them.

The simulator

Two snow routines, real station weather

WaterSim’s degree-day routine (exact against a hand calculation; equal to TUWmodel to 1e-12 mm) and daily energy balance (terms exact against hand arithmetic) melt snow under real dev-station weather up to 2010, shifted to other climates. The snowpack feels slightly different weather than the gauge records. Snow parameters come from calibrations on the dev stations’ own pillows before 2011.

The twin test

Reading the twins

Each dev station’s calibrated energy-balance twin scores NSE 0.917 against its pillow in 2011–2025. The released network reads that twin at 0.914 and the real pillow at 0.888, with peaks 4% below the twin and 9% below the pillow. The first rungs showed the parameter prior, not the network, was the gap.
