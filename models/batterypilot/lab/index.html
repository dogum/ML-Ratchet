BatteryPilot

A grid battery that decides, hour by hour, when to charge and when to sell. It learned only from simulated Texas and California grids. Then it traded real ERCOT prices for 2023–2025 and real CAISO prices for 2024–2025, which it had never seen.

How it was built

A price simulator with scarcity in it

Batteries in Texas make most of their money in a few dozen hours a year, when reserves run thin and the price jumps from $25 to thousands. GridSim's dispatch gets the everyday price right but has no such spikes. So BatteryPilot's simulator adds the physics that makes them, and fits each new piece only on the development years 2019 and 2021.

Real inputsEIA-930 demand, its day-ahead forecast, wind, solar, hydro, nuclear and imports, and NASA POWER temperature from 2018, 2020 and 2022.
GridSim fleetThe real ERCOT (or CAISO) fleet of that year: heat-rate curves, daily fuel prices, random forced and planned outages.
Supply physicsGas turbines lose output in heat. Gas supply and instruments freeze below about −3 °C. Storage fleets flatten the net load.
ScarcityAn operating-reserve demand curve like ERCOT's ORDC turns thin reserves into price adders up to the cap.
Two marketsDay-ahead prices come from forecasts and the expected adder. Real-time prices come from what actually happened.
Policy searchThe network is trained by running the battery through 14–21 day windows of 165 simulated years and climbing the revenue.
adder = (VOLL − λ) × LOLP(R − X), LOLP(x) = 1 − Φ((x − μ)/σ), RT = min(λ + adder, cap)
λ = marginal cost of the merit-order stack · R = spare thermal + storage capacity · X = minimum contingency level

The policy

Every hour, a small network (2 × 128 units, 34,000 weights) reads only what a trader would know before the hour: the last 24 hours of real-time prices, today's day-ahead prices and tomorrow's once they clear at 14:00, the grid's own demand forecast, the temperature, the calendar, and the battery. It answers with one curve: what a stored MWh is worth at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 % full. That curve becomes the battery's offer for the hour, cleared against the real-time price, the way a battery bids into ERCOT's real-time market. Training runs the battery physics (losses, power and energy limits, wear) inside the loss, so the network learns its curve by trading, not by copying a teacher.

The ratchet

Every rung, kept or not

A rung is kept only if it beats the incumbent on a frozen simulated test set by more than 0.01 (capture of perfect hindsight) and does not lose more than 0.01 on the real dev years (ERCOT 2019 and 2021 at four hubs, CAISO Aug–Dec 2023 at two). When the simulator changed, the frozen set was rebuilt and every rung re-scored.

    Use it

    Run it on your own prices

    import batterypilot as bp
net = bp.PolicyNet('batterypilot_weights.npz')
bat = bp.Battery(P=1.0, duration_h=2.0)             # 1 MW, 2 h usable, 85 % round trip
cl  = bat.c_lin(path['T'])                         # wear cost, $ per MWh sold
ch, dis, soc, V = bp.run_policy(net, path, bat, cl) # hourly arrays: rt, da, df, T, hour, day, dow, doy
print(bp.settle(bat, path['rt'], ch, dis, 0.0, path['T'])['net'])

    The release (batterypilot-v1.0.zip) is numpy and scipy only: the model, weights, the four baselines, a demo on real exam prices that runs in seconds, the model card and every training script.
