# StarQuake — status (6 Nov 2026, v1.0)

- **What it is:** red-giant asteroseismology from a Kepler light curve. A small network decides whether solar-like oscillations are present, then reads νmax (where the oscillation hump peaks) and Δν (the spacing of the comb of modes), each with an error bar. With an effective temperature, the scaling relations turn those into mass and radius. It was trained only on simulated stars; no real star was ever a training example. Sixth project in Greg's physics-ML collection.

## Recipe as built
- **Truth:** Yu et al. 2018 (ApJS 236, 42), 16,094 Kepler red giants, from the journal's machine-readable tables.
  - Exam: 420 stars, 70 in each of six νmax strata from 8 to 250 μHz (seed 2026), each with at least 1,200 days of data.
  - Dev: 18 other stars (3 per stratum).
  - Each star is also read from 365, 90, 54 and 27-day pieces of its own light curve.
- **Simulator** (`starsim.py`):
  - Granulation: two Kallinger et al. 2014 super-Lorentzians.
  - Oscillation envelope: Gaussian, width from Mosser et al. 2012, amplitude from Yu et al. 2018 eq. 8.
  - Modes: universal red-giant pattern for l=0–3 (Mosser et al. 2011), with ε(Δν), δ01, δ02, δ03 and curvature.
  - Dipole mixed modes from the asymptotic coupling formula, with ΔΠ1 and q drawn for RGB or clump.
  - Linewidths; stochastic excitation (χ² with 2 dof per bin); sinc² apodisation.
  - Δν comes from mass, Teff and νmax through the scaling relations, plus 2% scatter.
  - Signals are generated by inverse FFT and **added to real Kepler light curves of quiet dwarfs** of matching magnitude. Dwarfs oscillate above the 283 μHz Nyquist frequency, so these light curves are real noise and real gaps. 232 noise stars are used for training and 42 for validation, after a variability screen.
- **Physics checks** (`physics_check.json`):
  - χ² statistics: mean 1.004 and variance 1.001 (expected 1 and 1).
  - Parseval: variance ratio 0.988–0.990.
  - A classical pipeline recovers the inputs of 24 simulated stars to 1.4% (νmax) and 0.6% (Δν).
  - Our formulas reproduce Yu's 16,094 masses and radii (ratio 1.000; scatter 0.26% and 0.04%, from rounding).
  - Simulated Δν–νmax slope is 0.764 (Yu catalogue 0.739, Mosser 0.751).
  - The Sun gives 3090 / 135.1 μHz. This holds by construction of the references (5777 K, as in Yu; the IAU value of 5772 K changes νmax by 0.04%).
- **Calibration on dev (twin test):**
  - Simulated twins of the 18 dev stars showed the v1 humps 0.24 dex too tall. Yu's amplitudes use the Kjeldsen 2008 convention (A = √2 × rms).
  - v2 fixed this and fitted the hump width on dev. The residual is now −0.03 dex.
  - Network twin test (r1): median |error| on the real dev stars is 1.6% / 0.9%, against 1.2% / 1.1% on their twins (νmax / Δν).
- **Network:**
  - Two CNN branches, 412k parameters: a 1-D branch on the log spectrum (1024 log bins, 3–283 μHz) and a 2-D branch on a 48×110 "comb map" (autocorrelation around trial centres, lags in units of the expected Δν).
  - Two scalars: log observing length and duty cycle.
  - Loss: β-NLL on ln νmax and ln Δν, plus BCE on detection.
- **Ratchet:**
  - Objective: mean of the νmax-within-5% rate, the Δν-within-2% rate and the detection balanced accuracy, on the frozen val2 set (5,000 giants and 2,000 non-oscillating examples, v2 physics).
  - A rung is kept if it gains more than 0.01 AND the mean ratio of its four dev median errors to the incumbent's is at most 1.10.
  - When the physics changed from v1 to v2, every rung was re-scored and every decision re-taken.

| Rung | Change | Objective | Dev errors (whole νmax, Δν; 90 d νmax, Δν) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| r1 | v1 physics, 15k sims | 0.593 | 1.60 / 0.89 / 1.35 / 2.15% | kept (first) |
| r2 | v2 dev-calibrated physics, 15k | 0.584 | 1.66 / 1.20 / 2.49 / 2.00% | rejected: score down, dev +29% |
| r3 | + detection output, v2 + 30% non-oscillating, 25k | 0.738 | 1.65 / 1.32 / 2.61 / 1.80% | rejected: dev +32% |
| r4 | warm start from r1 + detection, all 40k (v1 + v2 physics) | 0.737 | 1.28 / 1.16 / 1.71 / 2.05% | kept (dev +8%); gain came from detection only |
| r5 | r4 trained 4,000 more steps | 0.757 | 1.01 / 0.78 / 2.34 / 1.57% | **kept, released** (dev −10%) |

The calibrated physics (v2) alone did not help the network. Mixing v1 and v2, which widens the amplitude prior, and training longer did help.

## Exam (r5; 420 stars; never used for any decision)

Each cell is the median |error| / the fraction of stars within 5% (νmax) or 2% (Δν). Coverage counts stars within 1σ, where σ adds the network error bar and the published error in quadrature, with widening factors fitted on dev (νmax ×1.00, Δν ×1.43).

- **Dumb baseline** (always guess the dev median): 77% νmax and 56% Δν median error.
- **Classical baseline:** a SYD-style pipeline (background fit, smoothed power excess, autocorrelation). Its v2 multi-start fit for pieces under 300 days was chosen on dev.
- **Detection** (420 giants vs 120 unscreened quiet dwarfs):

| Data | StarQuake: giants found | StarQuake: false alarms | Classical comb test: giants found | Classical: false alarms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 yr | 99.8% | 11.7% | 96.9% | 4.2% |
| 27 d | 100% | 14.2% | 39.5% | 38.3% |

  The classical test's threshold was chosen on dev.
- **Mass and radius** (whole light curve, Yu's Teff):
  - Median |difference| from Yu is 4.5% in mass and 1.9% in radius (bias +3.0% and +1.1%). Median error bars are 9.0% and 3.8%.
  - Against Gaia DR3 FLAME radii (222 stars), R/R_Gaia is 1.076 (scatter 7.1%). Yu's own radii give 1.063 (8.1%) and the classical pipeline 1.055 (8.7%). This is the known offset of the uncorrected scaling relations.

## Limits
- On 4-year light curves the classical pipeline wins. The truth catalogue was made with a similar pipeline, which favours it.
- Δν from 27 days: only 23% of stars are within 2%, and 46% are off by more than 5% (often by one comb tooth).
- Error bars are too narrow on short pieces: 1σ coverage is 54–58% at 27 days. The widening factor was pooled over lengths; it should be fitted per length.
- 11–15% of unscreened dwarfs are falsely called oscillating.
- The most luminous giants (νmax 8–15 μHz) read about 4% high in νmax. Their twins show the same bias, so it comes from the network.
- Kepler long cadence only. No TESS yet.

## Next rungs
1. A per-length (or per-observing-time) σ widening, and a νmax-dependent bias correction learned on simulations and checked on dev.
2. A TESS version: retrain on TESS FFI/SPOC quiet-star noise (27-day sectors with gaps) and examine on Hon et al. 2021 / Stello et al. 2022 TESS giants via MAST HLSP.
3. More training and a bigger bank. r5 shows the network is still under-trained (+0.02 from 4,000 more steps). Also hard negatives (spotted and eclipsing dwarfs) to cut false alarms.
