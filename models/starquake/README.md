# StarQuake <sub>v1.0</sub>

> **Weighing stars by listening to them ring.**

*How massive and how big is this red giant, from the way its surface oscillates?*

From a Kepler light curve, StarQuake decides whether a red giant's solar-like oscillations are present, then reads where the oscillation hump peaks (νmax) and the spacing of its comb of modes (Δν), each with an error bar. With a temperature, scaling relations turn those into mass and radius. It was trained only on simulated stars laid into real light curves of quiet dwarfs.

[Interactive lab](https://dogum.github.io/ML-Ratchet/models/starquake/lab/) · [Project page](https://dogum.github.io/ML-Ratchet/models/starquake/) · [Model card](MODEL_CARD.md) · [Lab notebook](NOTEBOOK.md) · [The method](https://github.com/dogum/ML-Ratchet/blob/main/docs/method.md)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reads** | A Kepler long-cadence light curve (any length from 27 days to 4 years), plus an effective temperature for mass and radius. |
| **Returns** | Oscillations detected (yes/no)<br>νmax and Δν (μHz), each with an error bar<br>Mass and radius (solar units) with error bars, via the scaling relations |
| **Learned from** | Two super-Lorentzian granulation components, a Gaussian oscillation envelope, the universal red-giant mode pattern for l = 0–3 with dipole mixed modes, linewidths and stochastic excitation, generated by inverse FFT and added to real Kepler light curves of quiet dwarfs (which oscillate above Nyquist, so their light curves are pure real noise and real gaps). |
| **Noise** | Real Kepler light curves of 232 quiet dwarfs (training) and 42 (validation), after a variability screen |
| **Network** | Two CNN branches, 412k parameters — 1-D on the log spectrum, 2-D on a comb map (autocorrelation in units of the expected Δν) |
| **Dev set** | 18 stars, 3 per νmax stratum |
| **Exam** | 420 stars, 70 in each of six νmax strata from 8 to 250 μHz (seed 2026), each read at 4 yr, 1 yr, 90, 54 and 27 days |

Coverage of the 68% error bars on the exam: νmax (4 yr) **72%**, Δν (4 yr) **78%**, νmax (27 d) **54%**, Δν (27 d) **58%**.

### Where it wins

- On short light curves (≤ 1 year) it beats the classical pipeline, by a lot at 27–90 days.
- Finds 100% of giants from 27 days against 39.5% for the classical comb test.

### Where it loses

- On 4-year light curves the classical pipeline wins — and the truth catalogue was made with a similar pipeline.
- Δν from 27 days is within 2% for only 23% of stars (often off by one comb tooth).

## Limits

- Error bars too narrow on short pieces (1σ coverage 54–58% at 27 days).
- 11–15% of unscreened dwarfs are falsely called oscillating.
- The most luminous giants (νmax 8–15 μHz) read about 4% high; their twins show the same bias.
- Kepler long cadence only; no TESS yet.

## Physics

Two super-Lorentzian granulation components, a Gaussian oscillation envelope, the universal red-giant mode pattern for l = 0–3 with dipole mixed modes, linewidths and stochastic excitation, generated by inverse FFT and added to real Kepler light curves of quiet dwarfs (which oscillate above Nyquist, so their light curves are pure real noise and real gaps).

**Simulator**

- Granulation from Kallinger et al. 2014; envelope width from Mosser et al. 2012; amplitudes from Yu et al. 2018
- Universal pattern (Mosser 2011) with ε(Δν), δ01, δ02, δ03 and curvature; mixed modes from ΔΠ1 and q
- χ² (2 dof) stochastic excitation and sinc² apodisation
- v2, calibrated on dev twins: humps 0.24 dex lower (Kjeldsen 2008 amplitude convention), width fitted on dev

**Checked against independent references**

- χ² statistics: mean 1.004, variance 1.001; Parseval ratio 0.988–0.990
- A classical pipeline recovers 24 simulated stars' inputs to 1.4% (νmax) and 0.6% (Δν)
- Reproduces Yu et al.'s 16,094 masses and radii (ratio 1.000)
